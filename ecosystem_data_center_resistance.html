The Material Politics of
Data Center Resistance

Bridging Computational Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) with Investigative Audio, Acoustic Fieldwork, and Grassroots Civic Action.

The Ecosystem Thesis: The unit of research in our working group is not an isolated 10-page student term paper. It is an interlocking public ecosystem where empirical datasets, algorithmic simulations, investigative podcasts, and community zines build on each other. Advanced students conduct rigorous computational social science with faculty, while first-year and qualitative students investigate the human and material stakes on the ground. Every piece makes the next piece more powerful.

THE DETTMERS APPRENTICESHIP PHILOSOPHY

Why We Build Ecosystems Instead of Assigning Papers

In an age of generative AI, writing a routine 10-page paper is no longer a proof of education. What cannot be automated is collaborative civic craft—standing in a community room with a microphone, conducting empirical audits of utility contracts, debating Deborah Stone's causal story theory with peers, and publishing open-source infrastructure for the public good.

1. For First-Year & Qualitative Students

You do not need to write code to do vital political science. Bring your curiosity, your visual eye, your voice, and your instinct for justice. You learn by doing: documenting public hearings, producing narrative podcasts, and authoring community guides.

2. For Advanced & Technical Students

Work side-by-side with faculty on real computational social science: fuzzy-set QCA, agentic deliberation simulations, spatial GIS data pipelines, and interactive web tools. You leave with published, open-source software and empirical research credentials.

3. The Reciprocal Feedback Loop

The quantitative models are sterile without the acoustic field recordings and human stories; the podcasts and zines are toothless without the 428-city dataset and Boolean proof. Every student depends on their peers' craft.
