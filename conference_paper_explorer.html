Technology Issue Field Expert Interview Project

Global Politics & California Policy • 45-Minute Recorded Interview & Policy Synthesis
PHASE 1

Conference Paper & Topic Selection

Review the curated conference papers in this explorer. Choose one paper whose theoretical puzzle speaks directly to an ongoing political or policy dilemma in either Global Politics (e.g., vaccine sovereignty, international digital colonialism, global minerals trade) or California State Politics (e.g., AI compute grid demands, SB 1047, Amazon Ring police surveillance, Lithium Valley, CARE Courts).

  • ✓ Use the "Select for My Project" button to bookmark your topic into your Project Workspace.
  • ✓ Identify the core tension between corporate technology claims and democratic/public interests.
PHASE 2

Technology Issue Deep-Dive & Literature Dossier

Before contacting any practitioner, you must develop rigorous baseline expertise on the issue. Experts will immediately recognize—and deeply respect—students who have done their homework.

  • ✓ Read at least two foundational academic sources (from the recommended readings list or scholar search queries).
  • ✓ Read at least one recent government policy report, legislative bill text, or white paper published within the last 24 months.
  • ✓ Draft a 1-page Background Dossier summarizing the technology's architecture, key financial interests, and governance loopholes.
PHASE 3

Expert Identification & Professional Cold Outreach

Identify a field expert working on the frontlines of your chosen technology issue. This can be a policy director at an advocacy organization, an investigative journalist, a regulatory agency staffer (e.g., CPPA, CPUC, CARB), a labor organizer, a public defender, or an academic researcher.

  • ✓ Use the customized Outreach Email Template provided in the paper's interview kit.
  • ✓ Request a 45-minute recorded Zoom session on a Friday or mutually agreeable time window.
  • ✓ Send outreach to 2–3 suitable candidates early to account for response times. Always maintain polite, professional follow-up etiquette.
PHASE 4

Conducting & Recording the 45-Minute Interview

Conduct the interview via Zoom or Riverside.fm with high-fidelity audio recording enabled. Guide the conversation through the 5-phase protocol provided in the app:

  • ✓ Recording & Informed Consent (0–2 min): State clearly on recording that the interview is conducted for academic research in political science at California Lutheran University and obtain verbal consent.
  • ✓ Phase 1: Empirical Grounding (5–8 min): Clarify the expert's role and the current state of technology implementation.
  • ✓ Phase 2: The Core Conflict (10–12 min): Probe the theoretical tension highlighted by the conference paper.
  • ✓ Phase 3: Power & Inequality (10–12 min): Investigate who profits, who bears externalized harms, and what labor/materials are hidden.
  • ✓ Phase 4: Global vs California Bridge (8–10 min): Explicitly ask the expert to bridge local California realities to global structures (or vice versa).
  • ✓ Phase 5: Democratic Solutions (5–8 min): Conclude by discussing viable statutory reforms, civil society resistance, or regulatory redesigns.
PHASE 5

Submission Deliverables

Upload your finished project package to Canvas:

  • ✓ Audio / Podcast File (MP3/WAV/Link): Cleanly recorded 40–45 minute audio file featuring an intro (student framing the topic for classmates) and outro.
  • ✓ Policy Synthesis Brief (3–4 pages): A formal policy memo connecting the expert's testimony to the conference paper's thesis and recommending concrete regulatory action in California or global governance.
  • ✓ Timestamped Index & Quotation Sheet: Key takeaways with exact audio timestamps.
📊 Project Grading Rubric (100 Points Total)
Evaluation Criterion Excellent (A: 90–100%) Proficient (B: 80–89%) Developing (C: 70–79%) Needs Work (D/F)
Research & Theoretical Preparation (25%) Deep mastery of the conference paper's core puzzle, related legislation, and literature. Questions demonstrate sharp insight into the expert's specific publications or advocacy. Clear familiarity with the issue and organization. Questions reference recent policy developments but may rely partly on generalized talking points. Basic surface-level understanding. Questions are somewhat generic or disconnected from the specific conference paper's theoretical problem. Superficial or improvised questions with no evidence of prior policy research or theoretical grounding.
Interview Execution & Question Depth (25%) Outstanding conversational pacing, active listening, and incisive follow-up questions. Adheres closely to the 45-minute protocol while naturally probing technical nuances. Smooth flow covering all required protocol stages. Good questions asked with occasional missed opportunities for organic follow-ups. Strictly reads off pre-written script with rigid transitions. Stilted pacing or ends significantly under/over the 45-minute mark. Unprepared, disorganized, unprofessional, or severely rushed execution. Fails to cover core conflict areas.
Global vs. California Policy Bridge (30%) Masterfully synthesizes the expert's testimony with broader structural governance. Seamlessly bridges global supply chains/treaties with California state legislation (e.g. SB 1047, CalRx, CEQA, CPRA). Effectively connects the expert's domain to either global or California politics, with a solid though less integrated comparative bridge. Touches upon California or global policy only briefly at the end without analytical integration into the rest of the discussion. Fails to establish any connection between the technology issue and California governance or international political economy.
Production Quality, Ethics & Synthesis Memo (20%) Crystal-clear audio with recorded intro/outro context. Verbal informed consent properly captured. Accompanying policy synthesis memo is rigorously argued and cited. Audible, clean audio with minor background hum. Solid policy memo connecting quotes to policy recommendations. Poor audio quality or missing intro framing. Policy synthesis memo is superficial or lacks specific citations.
