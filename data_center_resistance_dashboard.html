Rhetoric of Data Center Resistance

Research Question: Which combinations of Deborah Stone's causal stories (Intentional, Inadvertent, Mechanical, Accidental) and Narrative Policy Framework (NPF) elements (Moral Frames, Evidence Modes, Character Casting) are sufficient to produce positive attitude shifts (ΔY ≥ +1.50) toward municipal data center moratoriums across distinct empirical clusters of the U.S. public?

Study Logic & Theoretical Framework

Computational Narrative Policy Framework, in-silico deliberation, and Boolean minimization cross-validated against 428 municipal moratoriums

In public policy debates, how a speaker tells a story matters just as much as the facts on the ground (Jones & McBeth, 2010; Shanahan et al., 2011). To model this systematically, we treated persuasion like a structured recipe made of interchangeable ingredients rather than free-form rhetoric. First, we used Stone’s (1989) Causal Stories, or rhetorical devices that explain who or what is to blame for a problem: an Intentional Harm (a corporation knowingly exploiting a town), an Inadvertent Consequence (well-meaning policies that caused unexpected side effects), a Mechanical Breakdown (an unguided technological system running out of human control), or an Accidental Occurrence (an unforeseeable external shock). Next, we drew rhetorical elements from the Narrative Policy Framework (NPF) (McBeth et al., 2014; Shanahan et al., 2018). These included: Moral Frames (Graham et al., 2013; Haidt, 2012) like fairness, individual liberty, property rights, or environmental protection; distinct character roles like heroes, villains, and innocent victims; and evidence modes like emotional lived testimonials from neighbors versus dry scientific data and legal precedents.

Advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) allow us to create a population of simulated individuals from broad survey data (Argyle et al., 2023; Horton, 2023). We can use these profiles to test out different narrative strategies. In this brief, we used Google’s Gemini 1.5 Pro (Argon) to test different narrative framing strategies with respect to how they might influence public opposition to hyperscale data center developments. With Argon’s help, we constructed a Simulated Polity by ingesting microdata from the 2022 NORC General Social Survey (GSS; N = 3,784) (Smith et al., 2022). Using K-Means Clustering across sociodemographic indicators, institutional trust, and ideological priors, we identified four distinct Citizen Archetypes: Educated Progressive Conservationist, Traditional Pro-Growth Property Conservative, Working-Class Community Defender, and Conservative Conservationist and Ratepayer Skeptic.

By systematically mixing and matching one option from each category—like building a customized argument block by block—we produced an exact library of 1,600 unique narrative permutations, allowing us to mathematically test which specific combinations actually persuade different types of everyday citizens. We ran 500 simulated public hearing episodes across the bounded set (a Discrete Combinatorial Action Space) of all 1,600 possible strategy combinations (pairing one of 4 causal stories, one of 5 moral frames, and one of 4 evidence types equals 1,600 distinct narrative permutations).

To test which strategies would be more effective in shifting attitudes on data center construction, we employed Qualitative Comparative Analysis (QCA) (Ragin, 1987, 2008). QCA is a technique that looks for Chains of Causality rather than statistical correlation. The intuition of QCA is that there are multiple paths towards explaining an event (Equifinality). In this study, we looked at the groupings of different narrative strategies that were most likely to change the opinion of the simulated groups using Quine-McCluskey Boolean Minimization (Schneider & Wagemann, 2012).

In the final step, QCA-Boolean Chains were Cross-Validated against a dataset of 428 U.S. municipal and county data center moratoriums (data_center_moratoriums.csv). The dataset includes public hearing transcripts, local reporting, and statutory ordinance language from each jurisdiction. With the help of Gemini Argon, each case was coded into one of Deborah Stone’s four Causal Stories and Narrative Policy Framework elements. Localities were mapped to their corresponding NORC GSS Demographic Archetypes from the simulations using local Census and voting data.

We found that the minimal persuasive recipes predicted in the simulation directly mirrored the rhetoric that successfully enacted real-world policy pauses: progressive jurisdictions (e.g., Prince William County, VA) mobilized around Intentional Stories challenging closed-door NDAs and zoning buffers, while conservative rural boards (e.g., Fulton County, IN and Jerome Township, OH) enacted moratoriums only when rhetoric bypassed abstract climate claims to focus on Mechanical Utility Tariff Equity and Private Property Rights threatened by transmission-line eminent domain.

At present, my student team and I are in the process of validating Argon’s findings through human-coder inter-rater reliability testing against official city council and county board meeting minutes.

2. Methodology

Survey clustering, narrative configuration design, public hearing simulation, and Boolean minimization
Step 1

Survey Clustering (NORC GSS 2022)

Clusters N=3,784 complete NORC GSS 2022 respondents across polviews, partyid, degree, coninc, xnorcsiz, conbus, confed, consci, and natenvir using standardized K-Means (K=4).

download_gss.py + extract_archetypes.py
Step 2

Narrative Configuration Design

Defines 1,600 discrete combinations of Deborah Stone's 4 Causal Stories and Narrative Policy Framework (NPF) elements (Heroes, Villains, Victims, Moral Frames, Evidence Modes, and Tones).

action_space.py + narrative_realizer.py
Step 3

Public Hearing Simulation

Evaluates pre-treatment moratorium support (Y_pre on a 1–7 scale) and post-treatment shift (ΔY = Y_post - Y_pre) across 500 simulation episodes (125 per cluster), with threshold ΔY ≥ +1.50.

persona_environment.py + simulate_episodes.py
Step 4

Qualitative Comparative Analysis (QCA)

Applies Charles Ragin's Quine-McCluskey truth-table minimization (consistency ≥ 0.75) to extract minimal sufficient Boolean formulas and cross-tabulates against 428 municipal moratoriums.

qca_analyzer.py + data_center_moratoriums.csv
