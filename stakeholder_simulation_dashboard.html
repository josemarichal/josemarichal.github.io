Breaking Consent-Calendar Momentum: A Double-Blind Simulation of Narrative Framing in Municipal Surveillance Debates

Using an orthogonal two-layer agent architecture, this study tests which of Deborah Stone’s 10 causal stories and symbolic devices (Policy Paradox) can move an affluent suburban City Council (MHI > $115,000) to terminate a recurring Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) surveillance contract without triggering a veto-player backlash.

1. The Research Question & Methodological Problem

Why do public-comment campaigns against municipal surveillance contracts almost always fail in affluent suburbs—and how can we test alternative rhetorical strategies using AI agents without falling into prompt circularity?

Solving the "Tautological Recursion" Trap in LLM Social Science

In naive LLM policy simulations, researchers often construct stakeholder personas and experimental pitches using the exact same theoretical vocabulary inside a single prompt—causing the model to "grade its own homework." This study enforces strict theoretical orthogonality and double-blind agent separation across four automated pipeline stages:

1. Orthogonal Character Constructor (Sabatier’s ACF)

The 8 fixed municipal personas are constructed strictly via Paul Sabatier’s Advocacy Coalition Framework (ACF)—anchoring each actor in Deep Core Beliefs (normative, immovable), Policy Core Beliefs, and Secondary Instrumental Aspects (budgets, statutory compliance, hardware placement). Zero Deborah Stone concepts appear in the persona files.

2. Pure Rhetorical Treatments (Deborah Stone)

The 10 experimental treatments are drawn exclusively from Deborah Stone’s Policy Paradox: four Causal Stories (Intentional, Inadvertent, Mechanical, Accidental), three Narrative Arcs (Decline, Stymied Progress, Helplessness & Control), and three Symbolic Devices (Synecdoche, Metaphor, Strategic Ambiguity).

3. Double-Blind Arena & Deterministic Auditor

The Arena never sees the strategy name or theoretical mechanism—it receives only the unlabeled 100-word podium speech. Furthermore, the LLM never calculates the Political Power Index (PPI); The Auditor deterministically computes weighted PPI and Veto-Player neutrality in Python.

Stage 1 • The Hydrator

Frozen Suburban Control Arena

Initializes an anonymized Southern California contract-city (Pop. ~125,000, MHI > $115,000) considering whether to terminate a $275,000/yr ALPR camera lease and reallocate funds to neighborhood radar speed trailers, crosswalk lighting, and evening transit. Baseline Weighted PPI is 46.26%.

Stage 2 • The Strategist

10 Blinded Podium Speeches

Selects one of 10 Deborah Stone narrative strategies in a balanced Latin-square schedule (50 replications per strategy = 500 runs) and strips all theoretical labels, emitting only the raw public-comment testimony delivered at the council microphone.

Stage 3 • The Arena

ACF Belief-Tier Evaluation

Simulates how the 8 stakeholders react to the unlabeled speech. Attacks on Deep Core Beliefs trigger backlash among Key Veto-Players, whereas appeals that satisfy Secondary Instrumental Aspects unlock coalition crossovers.

Stage 4 • The Auditor

Deterministic PPI & Veto Check

Computes exact weighted PPI: Σ(Alignment × Weight) / 49. A run passes the Arena only if Final PPI ≥ 60%, all four Weight-10 Key Veto Players reach ≥ 40 Neutrality, and at least 3 of 4 Councilmembers vote Aye (≥ 50).

2. Four Substantive Findings from the 500 Simulations

Why standard activist framing fails in affluent suburbs—and which causal stories build a winning cross-coalition majority.

1. The "Digital Dragnet" Backlash (0% Pass Rate)

In national public-comment transcripts, the "Digital Dragnet / 1984 Slippery Slope" metaphor and Intentional Causal Blame ("corporate surveillance profiteers") are the most common activist frames. In an affluent suburb, however, they trigger a Deep Core Backlash among institutionalist Councilmembers (28 → 12) and HOA coalitions (25 → 13), who view perimeter checkpoints favorably. They preach to the choir while guaranteeing council defeat.

2. Flipping the Legal Gatekeeper (+26 Shift)

The Municipal City Attorney (Weight: 10, Base: 38) is completely unmoved by moral metaphors (-2 under Dragnet), but shifts +26 points (38 → 64) under a Mechanical Causal Story highlighting a 99.8% innocent-read ratio and unverified external agency query logs under California Civil Code § 1798.90.5 (SB 34), and +22 points (38 → 60) under Accidental Cause (third-party cloud breaches).

3. Lowest Variance of Failure: Inadvertent Cause (SD = 1.76)

Stone’s Inadvertent Causal Story—framing the $275k software lease as a well-intentioned past decision that unintentionally crowded out the radar speed trailers and crosswalk lighting requested by residents—saves face for senior institutionalists (28 → 44, Veto Neutralized) and achieves the lowest variance across all 500 runs.

4. Strategic Ambiguity as Coalition Glue (98% Pass Rate)

Framing contract termination as "Transitioning from Expiring Cloud Rentals to 100% City-Owned Visible Neighborhood Safety" achieves a 65.29% Mean PPI (+19.03%) and 100% Veto-Player Neutrality. Fiscal conservatives get city-owned hardware, HOAs get cul-de-sac speed trailers (+26), the City Attorney eliminates cloud liability (+20), and privacy advocates end plate logging.
