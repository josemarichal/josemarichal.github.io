1. Research Motivation & Authenticity Framework

Existing cross-platform political communication datasets—such as Qi, Dave, & Ling (2025), Adapting Public Personas: A Multimodal Study of U.S. Legislators' Cross-Platform Social Media Strategies (arXiv:2509.10720v2)—rely on TikTok's official API, which omits raw video visuals and forces researchers to analyze only captions and voice-to-text transcripts. Yet visual formality, camera staging, and production polish are central to how candidates perform authenticity.

This database preserves direct clickable tiktok_url links alongside pre-filled objective metadata so student coders can watch the actual videos and evaluate four authenticity strategies adapted from digital-branding research:

Strategy Type Typical Political Content & Staging Elements Perceived Authenticity
1. Relinquished Control / UGC-Style Handheld selfie-cam, car or kitchen-counter vlogs, off-camera Gen Z staffer interactions ("intern trend"), unscripted walk-and-talks, stitched/duetted voter Q&As. Very High
2. Trend-Jacking Campaign or candidate accounts adopting viral TikTok audio (is_original_sound = 0), CapCut templates, lip-syncs, or meme formats. Moderate to High
3. Campaign-Led Informational Direct-to-camera policy explainers, numbered legislative breakdowns, voting/deadline updates, town hall clips with moderate editing. Moderate
4. Polished Promotional Cross-posted horizontal TV ads, rally sizzle reels, drone B-roll, scripted teleprompter monologues, broadcast chyrons and stock music beds. Low

2. Three-Stage Collection Methodology

Stage 1 · Candidate Roster & Handle Discovery

Combined the 50-state OpenStates legislator dataset (7,323 sitting legislators) with 2026 Ballotpedia state-level general-election overviews across 16 states (12,075 total indexed candidates). Crawled candidate Ballotpedia profiles, official .gov pages, campaign websites, and Wikidata (P7085) with exponential backoff to discover 201 unique TikTok handles (253 account-candidate rows), tracking account_type (official, campaign, personal).

Stage 2 · Campaign-Window URL Archival

Ran yt-dlp (2026.10.26) across all deduplicated handles (up to 100 recent videos per account, 90s per-account timeout), storing 2,643 videos in SQLite and flagging 745 posts published between 2026-06-03 and 2026-11-15 (matching Qi, Dave, & Ling's Nov 15 election-window endpoint).

Stage 3 · Pre-Filled Student Coding Sheet

Exported student_coding_view to CSV and clickable HTML with objective fields pre-populated (posted_at, duration_sec, sound_title, is_original_sound, views, likes, comments, shares) alongside blank rubric columns (coder_id, strategy_type, perceived_authenticity_1_5, relinquished_control_yn, coding_notes).

Methodological Caveats for Student Coders

  • Posting-volume skew: Consistent with Qi, Dave, & Ling (2025), a small subset of highly active candidates accounts for a disproportionate share of videos. When assigning coding batches, stratify or cap videos per candidate so individual "super-posters" do not skew aggregate averages.
  • Platform extraction limits: Some discovered handles returned private-account errors, zero posted videos, or Unable to extract secondary user ID / 90s timeouts during automated extraction.
  • Party label completeness: Ballotpedia overview tables frequently encode party via cell styling rather than text; OpenStates-matched rows carry full party labels, while some Ballotpedia-only rows leave party blank or Other.
