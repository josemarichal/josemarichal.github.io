Automated Policing & The
Privatized Municipal Street

Connecting National Council Transcript Mining and Census ACS Equity Analytics with Investigative Audio, Street-Level Audits, and Citizen Testimony Toolkits.

The Ecosystem Thesis: Privatized automated license plate readers (ALPR) are quietly redrawing the civic geography of American cities. Rather than producing one-off term papers, our working group builds an interlocking public research ecosystem: faculty and advanced students stream national council transcripts through Deborah Stone metaphor classifiers and Census socioeconomic cross-tabs, while first-year and qualitative students record community interviews, document camera locations, and design practical public-comment toolkits for residents.

EMPIRICAL NLP • CENSUS ACTIVE RESEARCH

National Deliberation & Census Equity Dataset

Streaming Extraction of 3,581 Council Snippets • 100% Census ACS Income Matched across 270 Jurisdictions.

TRACK: Track A (Advanced Empirical Research)
RESEARCHER: Dr. Jose Marichal [Active / Published]
INPUTS: Harvard LocalView V3 • US Census ACS 5-Yr
STATUS: Live • Fully Published

By streaming 5.1 GB of municipal transcripts directly in memory without disk bloat, this pipeline isolated 3,581 Flock debate snippets and classified 124 Deborah Stone policy metaphors across 270 cities. Cross-referencing against Census ACS poverty data revealed an unsettling equity gap: affluent suburbs debate privacy in 98% procedural routine, while cash-strapped working-class cities deploy cameras as an austerity substitute for municipal labor.

AGENTIC SIMULATION ACTIVE RESEARCH

N=500 Stakeholder Policy Simulation

Orthogonal Monte Carlo Experiment on Municipal ALPR Contract Termination across Sabatier ACF Coalitions.

TRACK: Track A (Advanced Computational Research)
RESEARCHER: Dr. Jose Marichal & Student Co-Authors
INPUTS: Deborah Stone Causal Taxonomy • ACF Priors
STATUS: Live • Interactive Simulator

A double-blind, orthogonal 500-run Monte Carlo experiment testing which of Deborah Stone's 10 causal stories and symbolic devices can overcome consent-calendar momentum to terminate a municipal ALPR surveillance contract across divided city council coalitions.

DISCOVERED INTERVENTION MECHANISM: P < 0.001 SIGNIFICANT
INTENTIONAL_HARM_FRAME • UNPROTECTED_DATA_SHARING_EVIDENCE → TERMINATION_ODDS +340%

Finding: General privacy appeals fail to stop contract renewals. The only rhetorical framing that reliably terminates ALPR contracts is demonstrating out-of-state data sharing violating local sanctuary protections.

[SAMPLE] Southern California Intersection Audit

Photographic Ground-Truth Mapping of Solar-Powered ALPR Clusters Across Census Tracts.

TRACK: Track B (Fieldwork & Visual)
ROLE: Student Field Investigator Role Open
INPUTS: Physical Audits • Public Works Permits
STATUS: Sample Concept / Prototype
// SAMPLE STUDENT ARTIFACT • Available for undergraduates interested in urban planning, photography, and local field research.

ALPR cameras are intentionally designed to fade into urban infrastructure. Mounted on matte black poles with small solar panels, residents drive past them every day without realizing their license plates are logged into multi-agency networks. This fieldwork project documents the ground-truth locations and photographic evidence of camera siting across Ventura and LA counties.

THE DETTMERS APPRENTICESHIP PHILOSOPHY

Research as Living Civic Infrastructure

Why should student research stay locked behind course management systems? In the Technology and Society Working Group, every undergraduate artifact contributes to a public civic commons that journalists, city council members, and community organizers can actually use.

Qualitative & Creative Track

Learn investigative interviewing, podcast production, FOIA records excavation, and community zine illustration. No coding required.

Computational & Policy Track

Co-author national corpus extractions, Census socioeconomic cross-tabs, and agentic Monte Carlo simulations with faculty.

Collaborative Lineage

Work produced in year one becomes the foundation for future cohorts, creating a durable portfolio of published work.
