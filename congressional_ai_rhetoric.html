1. Research Questions & Theoretical Architecture NPF & CAUSAL STORIES

Congressional hearings serve as the primary institutional crucible where emerging technologies are translated from technical abstraction into actionable policy problems. When artificial intelligence is debated on Capitol Hill, legislators and expert witnesses do not merely describe technical benchmarks; they construct policy narratives designed to allocate moral blame, define victims, celebrate heroes, and justify either government intervention or deregulation.

This project investigates the narrative structure of Congressional AI discourse through two complementary political science frameworks:

Deborah Stone (1989) • Mechanical Story

Mechanical Causal Stories

Consequences Intended / Actions Unguided: Treating AI as a deterministic, runaway machine operating without human intervention.

Congressional Framing: "Algorithmic black boxes," autonomous weapons targeting loops, hallucinating LLMs, and opaque credit/hiring bots. Blame is placed on the mechanical apparatus itself, with calls for engineering circuit-breakers, technical audits, and automated watermarking.

Deborah Stone (1989) • Inadvertent Story

Inadvertent Causal Stories

Consequences Unintended / Actions Guided: Well-meaning actors deploying AI for efficiency or growth, inadvertently generating systemic societal harms.

Congressional Framing: Unforeseen algorithmic discrimination in tenant screening, training-data ingestion of copyrighted creative works, and inadvertent workforce displacement. Blame is mitigated as an "unfortunate side effect" requiring gentle regulatory guardrails or workforce reskilling.

Deborah Stone (1989) • Intentional Story

Intentional Causal Stories

Consequences Intended / Actions Guided: AI harms framed as purposeful exploitation by self-interested or malicious actors.

Congressional Framing: Big Tech platform monopolization, predatory deepfake child exploitation, intentional wage theft, algorithmic price-fixing, and foreign adversary cyber warfare (e.g., CCP and Russian disinformation campaigns). Demands criminal penalties, antitrust breakups, and strict export controls.

Stone & NPF • Accidental Story

Accidental & Competitive Frames

Consequences Unintended / Actions Unguided: Random technological shocks, stochastic hallucinations, or unavoidable global races.

Congressional Framing: Framing AI supremacy as an existential geopolitical race against China where any domestic regulation is framed as unilateral disarmament. The "hero" is the domestic startup; the "villain" is regulatory overreach.

Key Partisan & Institutional Findings

  • Partisan Divergence: Republican Members disproportionately frame AI through Strategic Competition & Innovation Primacy (focusing on China competition, red-tape elimination, and military superiority), whereas Democratic Members concentrate heavily on Inadvertent & Intentional Harms (algorithmic bias, deepfake election manipulation, algorithmic wage suppression, and copyright infringement).
  • Committee Jurisdiction as Narrative Prism: Senate Commerce & House Science focus on technical verification and NIST standards; Senate Judiciary centers IP theft, synthetic likeness rights, and section 230 carve-outs; Senate HELP interrogates automated workplace surveillance and job displacement; and Senate Armed Services & Intelligence focus on autonomous cyber operations and sovereign foundation models.
  • Witness Sector Insulation: Industry witnesses predominantly rely on Inadvertent Causal Stories (promising self-regulatory safety frameworks while warning that strict liability would stifle domestic competitiveness), whereas academic and civil society witnesses demand Mechanical Transparency and statutory algorithmic impact assessments.

2. Corpus Architecture & Lexical Taxonomy LEXICAL DISTRIBUTION

The corpus was compiled using the official GPO GovInfo API across all hearing packages (`CHRG`) published between November 15, 2023, and November 15, 2026. Transcripts were diarized into speaker turns, enriched with legislator metadata from unitedstates/congress-legislators, and mapped against witness witness rosters.

GUIDE-LLM AI Transparency Checklist

Reporting standards for generative and agentic AI in computational social science research (Feuerriegel et al., 2026, Nature Human Behaviour, DOI: 10.1038/s41562-026-02492-7).

Dimension GUIDE-LLM Requirement Project Implementation & Verification
1. Model Specification Model family, architecture, version, release date Corpus extraction and regex diarization performed via deterministic Python scripts (Python 3.11/3.13, Beautiful Soup, Pandas). Code assistance and dashboard compilation facilitated by Antigravity (Gemini 2.5 Flash / Pro).
2. Prompt Engineering Full prompt text, system prompts, few-shot examples GovInfo API queries used deterministic boolean logic: collection:CHRG AND publishdate:range(2023-11-15,2026-11-15) AND ("artificial intelligence" OR "generative AI" OR "large language model" OR "foundation model" OR "machine learning" OR deepfake OR algorithmic OR "AI").
3. Hyperparameters Temperature, top-p, seed, penalties, context window Not applicable for deterministic regex and Bioguide roster matching; zero hallucination risk in data harvesting.
4. Data Preprocessing Tokenization, cleaning, truncation, encoding HTML hearing transcripts stripped of line-numbering and formatting artifacts; regex-based speaker detection anchored to Congressional honorific titles and Senate/House presiding officer tokens.
5. Validation & Evaluation Human audit, inter-rater reliability, benchmarks $N = 200$ stratified preliminary sample manually coded across Deborah Stone's 4 causal story categories to establish inter-coder agreement benchmarks before full-corpus scaling.
6. Reproducibility Code availability, public seeds, environment specs All harvester scripts (`01_fetch_govinfo.py`, `02_prepare_corpus.py`) and datasets are mirrored directly in the open GitHub repository.
