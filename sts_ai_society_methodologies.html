Methodological Approaches in Technology & Society Research

How do scholars empirically capture, deconstruct, and challenge algorithmic power? This compendium outlines the six dominant methodological paradigms deployed across top peer-reviewed venues (New Media & Society, Big Data & Society, AI & Society, and Information, Communication & Society), mapping their epistemological assumptions, canonical citations, triangulation strategies, and 2024–2026 frontiers.

JUMP TO: 1. Algorithmic Auditing 2. Trace Ethnography 3. Computational Social Science 4. Critical Discourse (CDA) 5. Survey Experiments 6. Speculative & Arts-Led • Network Map • Journal Matrix • Current Trends

Part 1: Top Methodological Approaches & Canonical Examples

// 6 CORE PARADIGMS
METHODOLOGY 01

Algorithmic Auditing & Forensic Interface Analysis

EPISTEMOLOGY: Empirical Verification • Reverse Engineering • Critical Software Studies

Auditing methods inspect how platforms, recommender engines, and automated decision-making (ADM) systems behave under controlled empirical conditions. Because commercial platforms conceal their algorithmic weights as trade secrets, researchers treat systems as "black boxes," systematically measuring inputs, outputs, and interface affordances to uncover discriminatory bias, radicalization dynamics, or extraction mechanics.

The Walkthrough Method

Systematically engages with an application or interface step-by-step—from entry (onboarding, permissions), to everyday use (core interface, algorithmic prompts), to suspension/exit—to unpack its embedded cultural biases, governance, and business models.

Canonical Reference: Light, B., Burgess, J., & Duguay, S. (2018). "The walkthrough method: An approach to analyzing apps." New Media & Society, 20(3), 881–900.

Sock-Puppet / Audit Studies

Researchers create coordinated synthetic accounts (bots/personas) with randomized or controlled attributes (e.g., gender, race, ideological leaning, browsing history) to empirically measure differential treatment, ad pricing disparities, or rabbit-hole radicalization.

Canonical Reference: Bandy, J. (2021). "Problematic Machine Behavior: A Systematic Literature Review of Algorithm Audits." Big Data & Society, 8(1), 1–18.
// Working Group Application: In our Flock Camera Surveillance Ecosystem, we adapt audit logic to the physical street: mapping automated license plate reader (ALPR) placement density against Census median household income tracts to audit privatized municipal surveillance.
METHODOLOGY 02

Digital, Platform, & Trace Ethnography

EPISTEMOLOGY: Interpretive • Phenomenological • Situated Knowledge

Adapts traditional sociological and anthropological participant observation to environments where human interaction is recorded, mediated, and altered by software architectures. It prioritizes the lived experience of human actors navigating, resisting, or rationalizing computational systems.

Algorithmic & Trace Ethnography

Blends participant observation with digital server logs, revision histories, and computational traces to understand how communities interact with automated governance and develop "folk theories" about how opaque algorithms rank them.

Canonical References: Bucher, T. (2017). "The algorithmic imaginary: Exploring the ordinary affects of being target of an algorithm." Information, Communication & Society.

Geiger, R. S., & Ribes, D. (2011). "Trace ethnography: Following coordination through computational and log data." HICSS / applied widely in BDS & NMS.

Virtual & Immersion Ethnography

Deep immersion in niche digital subcultures—such as rideshare driver subreddits, algorithmic trading Discord channels, or DAO governance forums—examining how workers build informal solidarity networks to subvert automated management.

Canonical Reference: Jarrahi, M. H., et al. (2021). "Algorithmic management in a work context." Big Data & Society, 8(2), 1–14.
// Working Group Application: In our Synthetic Media Ecosystem, we harvest 58 battleground state subreddits to trace how actual swing-state voters debate, detect, and react to AI-generated political campaign clips.
METHODOLOGY 03

Computational Social Science & Text-as-Data

EPISTEMOLOGY: Quantitative • Macro-Scale Structural • Network Theory

Large-scale quantitative collection and mathematical modeling of digital trace data. It integrates natural language processing (NLP), transformer embeddings, and social network analysis (SNA) to map macro-structural communication phenomena across millions of posts, articles, or transcripts.

Semantic & Topic Modeling (NLP / LLMs)

Deploys embedding spaces, Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), or LLM zero-shot classification across massive corpora to capture shifts in political rhetoric, synthetic campaign propaganda, and ideological polarization over time.

Canonical Reference: Hemsley, J., et al. (2018). "Social media and political communication." New Media & Society, 20(3), 850–870.

Network Topologies & Diffusion Analysis

Utilizes graph theory metrics (eigenvector centrality, Louvain modularity, k-core decomposition) to model how information cascades, astroturfed disinformation campaigns, and coordinated inauthentic behavior propagate across social networks.

Canonical Reference: Starbird, K., et al. (2019). "Disinformation as Collaborative Work: Surfacing the Participatory Nature of Strategic Information Operations." Proceedings of the ACM / BDS cross-citations.
// Working Group Application: In our streaming pipeline for the Flock Camera study, we ingested Harvard LocalView’s 37,655 council meetings directly in memory, extracting and classifying 3,581 debate snippets using zero-bloat NLP parsers.
METHODOLOGY 04

Critical Discourse Analysis & Sociotechnical Imaginaries

EPISTEMOLOGY: Critical Hermeneutic • Foucault • Political Economy

Favored heavily in AI & Society and Big Data & Society, this paradigm interrogates how power, hegemony, and racial/class domination are baked into institutional narratives, national policy whitepapers, tech marketing rhetoric, and foundational AI ethics charters.

Sociotechnical Imaginaries Framework

Drawing on Sheila Jasanoff and Sang-Hyun Kim, this method analyzes how nation-states, venture capital firms, and engineering elites construct collectively held visions of desirable social futures enabled by technological breakthroughs.

Canonical References: Bareis, J., & Katzenbach, C. (2022). "Talking AI into being: The narratives and imaginaries of AI in European national strategies." New Media & Society, 24(4), 855–875.

Jasanoff, S., & Kim, S.-H. (2015). Dreamscapes of Modernity: Sociotechnical Imaginaries and the Fabrication of Power. Univ. of Chicago Press.

Critical Racial & Postcolonial Deconstruction

Interrogates how artificial intelligence systems reproduce colonial extraction, linguistic hegemony, and racial hierarchy through datasets, marketing aesthetics, and corporate value alignment.

Canonical Reference: Cave, S., & Dihal, K. (2020). "The Whiteness of AI." Philosophy & Technology / cited frequently in AI & Society.
// Working Group Application: In our Data Center Resistance Ecosystem, we operationalize Deborah Stone’s policy causal stories (Accidental, Inadvertent, Mechanical, Intentional) to dissect how tech companies rebrand massive water/grid extraction as patriotic modernization.
METHODOLOGY 05

User-Centered Experimental & Survey Methods

EPISTEMOLOGY: Positivist • Behavioral • Causal Inference

Quantitative behavioral assessments measuring how humans perceive, trust, defer to, or resist automated decisions. Frequently deployed to study algorithmic aversion, moral delegation, and attitudes toward synthetic generated media.

Conjoint & Vignette Survey Experiments

Presents respondents with randomized, orthogonal variations of algorithmic attributes (e.g., human vs. AI author; automated vs. human moderation; corporate vs. public ownership) to causally isolate which variables drive user trust and compliance.

Canonical Reference: Köbis, N., et al. (2021). "The social dilemma of autonomous machines." AI & Society, 36(4), 1147–1158.

Algorithmic Fairness & Folk Theory Scales

Deploys psychometric and validated behavioral scales to measure how users conceptualize procedural vs. distributive justice in automated credit, parole, or hiring recommendations.

Canonical Reference: Shin, D. (2021). "The perception of algorithmic fairness: How users understand algorithms." New Media & Society, 23(8), 2277–2301.
// Working Group Application: In our Stakeholder Policy Analyst Dashboard, we run an orthogonal N=500 Monte Carlo simulation modeling how changing council framing alters city council voting coalitions under Sabatier’s Advocacy Coalition Framework.
METHODOLOGY 06

Speculative, Arts-Led, & Participatory Action Research

EPISTEMOLOGY: Posthumanist • Materialist • Agonistic Design

Particularly prominent in AI & Society, this methodology rejects computational reductionism. It deploys creative prototyping, speculative fiction, acoustic field recordings, and participatory community co-design to make the invisible material extraction and surveillance of AI systems physically tangible.

Speculative Design & Critical Prototyping

Constructs provocative, intentionally non-functional or satirical technological artifacts to expose the hidden corporate assumptions, labor exploitation, or surveillance logic embedded in normal tech products.

Canonical Reference: Frauenberger, C. (2020). "Entanglement: Designing in Posthumanist and New Materialist Conditions." AI & Society, 35(1), 33–44.

Participatory Action & Community Zines

Translates technical research into tactile community artifacts—such as risograph-printed zines, public comment guides, and workshops—arming residents to intervene in local planning and police oversight hearings.

Canonical Reference: Costanza-Chock, S. (2020). Design Justice: Community-Led Practices to Build the Worlds We Need. MIT Press.
// Working Group Application: Each of our research ecosystems incorporates student-illustrated community zines and 2-minute city council testimony templates so our academic findings directly empower everyday citizens at municipal podiums.

Part 2: Network Map of Methodological Clusters

// EPISTEMIC TRIANGULATION & HYBRIDS

Technology and Society scholarship rarely relies on a single isolated technique. Leading papers gain empirical traction by sitting at the intersections of three foundational epistemological pillars: Computational (large trace data), Qualitative / Ethnographic (lived experience), and Critical / Hermeneutic (power and political economy).

Part 3: Methodological Dynamics Across Specific Journals

// EDITORIAL PROFILES & TRIANGULATION

Each premier technology-and-society journal has cultivated a distinct methodological culture. Successful submissions align their research design with the venue's core epistemological expectations:

Journal & Impact Profile Dominant Methodological Profile Typical Methodological Triangulation Key Methodological Challenge Addressed
New Media & Society (NMS) SAGE • IF: ~5.0 • Elite Social Science Mixed Methods & Behavioral: Platform Walkthroughs, Conjoint Survey Experiments, Computational Framing, and Quantitative Behavioral Surveys. Combines large-N platform metric scraping with qualitative semi-structured interviews to ground behavioral claims in user experience. Overcoming commercial platform API shutdowns; establishing rigorous causal links between interface design and human behavioral shifts.
Big Data & Society (BDS) SAGE • Open Access • Critical Data Studies Critical Data Studies: Algorithmic Audits, Trace Ethnography, Infrastructure Studies, and Forensic Software Dissection. Integrates programmatic system audits with deep continental philosophy (e.g., Foucault, Deleuze, Zuboff, Postcolonial Theory). The algorithmic "black box" dilemma; reflexivity regarding academic researchers relying on commercial cloud infrastructure.
AI & Society Springer • Human-Centered / Critical Theory Human-Centered & Speculative: Sociotechnical Imaginaries, Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA), Phenomenological Critique, and Arts-Led Prototyping. Interweaves the philosophy of technology, historical genealogy, and speculative interaction design to challenge technical orthodoxy. Resisting computational reductionism; vigorously defending human dignity, labor agency, and social justice against Silicon Valley determinism.
Information, Communication & Society (ICS) Routledge • Political Communication Computational Social Science: Social Network Analysis (SNA), Political Disinformation Cascade Modeling, and Longitudinal Panel Surveys. Bridges automated NLP topic topologies with longitudinal social surveys to evaluate political polarization and democratic participation. Representativeness of digital trace data; accounting for platform migration biases (e.g., user exodus from X to Bluesky or TikTok).

Part 4: Frontier Methodological Trends (2024–Present)

// HOW THE DISCIPLINE IS EVOLVING

As commercial platforms erect severe paywalls and API restrictions, the methodology of technology and society research is undergoing a structural transformation. Three emergent practices represent the state of the art:

1. Synthetic Persona Testing (In-Silico Audits)

Rather than recruiting thousands of human participants for early-stage trials, researchers configure LLMs conditioned on empirical demographic priors (such as the NORC General Social Survey) to simulate multi-agent deliberation, measure synthetic algorithmic persuasion, and audit conversational search engines under repeatable conditions.

2. Post-API Data Collection Protocols

Following the shutdown of academic API tiers across X, Reddit, and Meta, scholars have pivoted toward client-side auditing: lightweight browser extensions, secure user data-donation protocols, and privacy-preserving data cooperatives that empower citizens to donate their algorithmic feeds directly to researchers.

3. Multi-Platform & Cross-Media Tracing

Disinformation, political deepfakes, and meme culture no longer reside in single walled gardens. Modern research protocols trace the cross-media trajectory of synthetic content as it originates on anonymous boards (4chan/Telegram), migrates to video platforms (TikTok/YouTube Shorts), and is recirculated into mainstream discourse.
