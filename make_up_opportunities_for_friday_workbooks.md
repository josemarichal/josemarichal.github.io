## Overview & Make-Up Protocol

Friday workbooks are interactive, in-class policy exercises designed to build practical skills in policy analysis, stakeholder mapping, and civil discourse. If you missed an in-class Friday workbook due to an excused or unavoidable absence, you may earn make-up credit (1 point per workbook, graded pass/fail) by attending an approved public lecture, author talk, or symposium and submitting a short reflection memo.

### Steps to Complete a Make-Up:
1. **Attend the Approved Event:** Attend the full event in person and take notes on the main arguments, evidence presented, and Q&A discussion.
2. **Write a Brief Reflection Memo:** Write a 1–2 page (350–500 word) reflection synthesizing the speaker's core claims and connecting them to concepts from class (e.g., Deborah Stone's Polis model, coalition dynamics, racial equity policy, or social movement theory).
3. **Submit on Canvas:** Submit your written memo to the "Friday Workbook Make-Up" portal on Canvas within one week of attending the event.

## Approved Event Options

### Option 1: Dr. Chandra Russo Book Talk
- **Speaker:** Dr. Chandra Russo (Associate Professor of Sociology, Colgate University)
- **Book / Topic:** *White Flank: Organizing White People for Racial Justice* (University of Minnesota Press)
- **Date:** Tuesday, November 11, 2026
- **Time:** 4:00 PM – 6:00 PM
- **Location:** Richter Hall / Ahmanson 100

#### Event Description:
Dr. Chandra Russo will discuss her latest book, *White Flank: Organizing White People for Racial Justice*. Her ethnographic and sociological research examines how white activists organize within and alongside multiracial movements, the politics of solidarity, accountability, and the tensions between internal movement education and external systemic change.

#### Guiding Questions for Your Reflection:
- **Movement Dynamics & Coalitions:** What strategic challenges and opportunities arise when building multiracial coalitions, and how does Dr. Russo define the role of a "white flank" in racial justice organizing?
- **Theoretical Integration:** How does this talk connect with course frameworks—such as Deborah Stone’s model of the *Polis* (shared loyalties, community values, and collective action) versus individualized market models?
- **Policy & Institutional Power:** How can grassroots social movements effectively pressure formal policy systems without having their core goals co-opted or diluted?

### Option 2: HIV/AIDS Public Health Symposium, Community Quilting Bee & Vigil
- **Organizers & Speakers:** Organized by **Dr. Adina Nack**; featuring **Dr. Uldine Castel, MD** (Ventura County Public Health Officer) and **Dr. Peter Carlson**
- **Date:** November 2026 (RSVP for light dinner by **11/10/26**)
- **Location:** California Lutheran University Campus
- **Format:** Three distinct segments (students may attend any or all segments):
  1. **Quilting Bee & Community Fair (4:05 – 5:15 p.m.):** Visit tables hosted by local organizations focused on HIV/AIDS, collect stickers on your "passport" to win a spin at the prize wheel, and enjoy a light dinner while you learn. *(RSVP required by 11/10/26)*
  2. **Public Health Reception (5:45 – 6:20 p.m.):** Dr. Adina Nack will host local experts, including Ventura County’s Public Health Officer, Dr. Uldine Castel, MD, who will share their experiences navigating the U.S. epidemic, followed by Q&A. *(No RSVP required)*
  3. **Keynote Panel & Vigil (6:30 – 7:30 p.m.):** Hear from a resident living with HIV/AIDS and join in a nondenominational vigil led by Dr. Peter Carlson to honor those living with HIV/AIDS and those who have passed. *(No RSVP required)*

#### Guiding Questions for Your Reflection:
- **Public Health Governance & Local Bureaucracy:** What challenges do county-level health officers face in addressing health disparities and coordinating services across regional health jurisdictions?
- **Grassroots Organizing & Mutual Aid:** How do community-based organizations and advocacy tables bridge the gap between formal government healthcare and vulnerable populations?
- **Lived Experience in the Polis:** How does firsthand narrative testimony from individuals living with chronic illness shape policy empathy, community solidarity, and political mobilization?
