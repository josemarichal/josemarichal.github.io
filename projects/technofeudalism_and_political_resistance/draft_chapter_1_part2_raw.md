## Ontological Enclosure
This detachment of liberalism from democracy has a technological antecedent. Part of the cynicism regarding the democratic possibilities of technology results from the failures of the last technological era. The current state of authoritarian surveillance technology is far from the proverbial “jetpacks” we were promised. Tech companies have more than earned the public’s distrust. The Internet of the early 2000’s foregrounded a participatory governance model (Siriani 2009). The metaphors used during this era emphasized individual and community empowerment. City governments were abuzz with the prospect of delivering more efficient city services while inviting citizens to create useful apps from public data. In 2010, then US President Obama Civic hacking proclaimed September 18 as the National Day of Civic Hacking. Books like Yochai Benkler’s The Wealth of Networks (2006),Henry Jenkins’ Convergence Culture (2006), Clay Shirky’s Here Comes Everybody (2008), Lawrence Lessig’s Remix (2008) were serious books that highlighted transformational challenges but were mostly sanguine about the democratic possibilities of the Internet.
Despite the initial presumption that Internet Culture would create greater openness and interconnection, key market actors pushed the technology in darker directions. Rather that “bring the world closer together,” the current iteration of tech tools has given users the ability to not only curate their information environments, but to structure their experience of the world, a concept I call ontological enclosure. Wells (2025), draws a distinction between Kantian/liberal universalist ethics and Marx’s ethic that depends upon one’s awareness of class position. Kant offers universalism as a proposition or as an axiom, illustrated by the categorical imperative. Every first year philosophy student learns the principle that we should apply our moral principles in the same way in all circumstances. This prinicples underlies the liberal view of the law as a neutral arbiter meting out blind justice.
