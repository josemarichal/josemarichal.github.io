Instructions

Print this workbook in its entirety. You must select a specific California policy area to focus on for the semester. Each week, bring this physical workbook to class and submit your handwritten responses to the weekly prompt for a pass/fail grade.

The deliverables below are aligned with the weekly course readings and themes. Use this workbook to build your policy reform strategy incrementally over the 16-week semester.

Week 1: The Polycrisis & The Scarcity Trap
Context: We are examining the "polycrisis" and the conflict between progressive abundance and physical/fiscal scarcity.
Prompt: Identify your policy area for the semester (e.g., Housing, Wildfires, Transit, Education). What is the central "scarcity trap" in this area? What specific reform or abundant outcome are you trying to achieve?
Week 2: Equity & Efficiency (Issue Spotting Memo)
Context: Stone Ch. 1, 2, & 3. The Market vs. the Polis; conflicts over Equity and Efficiency.
Prompt: Frame your chosen policy gap as an equity problem. Then, explain how opponents might use "efficiency" arguments to block your proposed structural reform.
Week 3: Federalism & State Constitutions
Context: OSU Ch. 1 & 2. The impact of federalism and state constitutional structure.
Prompt: How does federalism (state vs. federal vs. local power) impact your policy? Is your issue primarily governed by state law, federal law, or local ordinances?
Week 4: Constitutions & Numbers
Context: OSU Ch. 6, Stone Ch. 7. State Constitutions and the symbolic power of Numbers.
Prompt: How is your policy issue quantified? What "numbers" are used to define the problem? Are there state constitutional constraints that affect it?
Week 5: Key Actors & Interests
Context: OSU Ch. 5, Stone Ch. 9. Key actors in the policy process and organized interests.
Prompt: Map the interest groups in Sacramento. Who are the key actors opposing your reform, and what are their interests?
Week 6: Legislatures, Executives & Symbols
Context: OSU Ch. 7, 8, Stone Ch. 6. The Legislative Branch, Executive Branch, and Symbolic framing.
Prompt: How is your issue framed symbolically? How will you navigate the legislature and executive branch to advance your reform?
Week 7: Causes
Context: Stone Ch. 8. Causal stories in the Polis.
Prompt: What causal story (e.g., accidental, intentional, inadvertent) do opponents tell to justify the status quo of your issue?
Week 8: Bureaucracy, Powers, & Local Government
Context: OSU Ch. 10, 4, Stone Ch. 15. Bureaucracy, administrative powers, and local sustainability.
Prompt: Identify the specific state agency or bureaucracy responsible for your policy. What specific administrative powers can you leverage, and how might local governments try to block it?
Week 9: Budgeting & Decisions
Context: OSU Ch. 11, Stone Ch. 11. Budgeting, public finance, and policy decisions.
Prompt: Conduct a rough Cost-Benefit Analysis of your policy. How will you fund this reform during state budget cycles?
Week 10: Courts, Rules, & Welfare
Context: OSU Ch. 9, Stone Ch. 13, 4. The courts, legal rules, and social welfare.
Prompt: How could existing rules be weaponized in the courts to delay your policy? How does your policy address the welfare/security of Californians?
Week 11: Services & Liberty
Context: OSU Ch. 13, Stone Ch. 5. Public services and individual liberty.
Prompt: Does your policy expand or restrict individual liberty? How does it affect the delivery of visible public services?
Week 12: Rights, Powers, & The Margins
Context: Stone Ch. 14, OSU Ch. 3. Fundamental rights and the new margins.
Prompt: Frame your policy as a fundamental "right." How does this affect marginalized communities (The New Margins)?
Week 13: Persuasion & Entitlements
Context: Stone Ch. 10, OSU Ch. 12. Policy persuasion and entitlement structures.
Prompt: How will you persuade opponents to support your policy? Are there entitlement structures that need reform?
Week 14: Service Delivery Implementation
Context: OSU Ch. 13. Traditional and visible services.
Prompt: Finalize your implementation plan for state and local service delivery.
Week 15: Security, Causes, & Decisions
Context: Stone Ch. 5, 8, 12. Security, causal stories, and decision-making.
Prompt: Final reflection on the causal narrative of your policy journey and your proposed structural reforms.
Week 16: Final Reflection
Context: Navigating political power and finalizing the advocacy portfolio.
Prompt: Reflect on the simulation. What was the most difficult structural barrier to passing your policy in California? How did your strategy evolve over the past 16 weeks?
