Instructions

Print this workbook in its entirety. You must select a specific California policy area to focus on for the semester. Each week, bring this physical workbook to class and submit your handwritten responses to the weekly prompt for a pass/fail grade.

The deliverables below are aligned with the weekly course readings and themes. Use this workbook to build your policy reform strategy incrementally over the 16-week semester.

Week 1: The Polycrisis & The Scarcity Trap
Context: We are examining the "polycrisis" and the conflict between progressive abundance and physical/fiscal scarcity.
Prompt: Identify your policy area for the semester (e.g., Housing, Wildfires, Transit, Education). What is the central "scarcity trap" in this area? What specific reform or abundant outcome are you trying to achieve?
Week 2: Equity & Efficiency (Issue Spotting Memo)
Context: Stone Ch. 1, 2, & 3. The Market vs. the Polis; conflicts over Equity and Efficiency.
Prompt: Frame your chosen policy gap as an equity problem. Then, explain how opponents might use "efficiency" arguments to block your proposed structural reform.
Week 3: Numbers & Symbols (Direct Democracy)
Context: Stone Ch. 7. The California Initiative Process and Prop 13's legacy.
Prompt: How do data ("numbers") and narratives ("symbols") currently frame your policy area in the public consciousness? Draft a short narrative that re-frames the issue using a new, compelling symbol.
Week 4: Interests & Causes (Legislative Mapping)
Context: Stone Ch. 6, 8, & 9. The Legislative Branch and Sacramento's Key Actors.
Prompt: Map the interest groups in Sacramento. Who are the primary organized interests opposing your reform, and what causal story (accidental, intentional, inadvertent) do they tell to justify the status quo?
Week 5: Powers & Bureaucracy (Administrative Action)
Context: Stone Ch. 15. The Executive Branch and State Capacity.
Prompt: Identify the specific state agency, department, or executive power that would be responsible for implementing your policy. How can you pressure or petition this agency to act?
Week 6: Local Government Vetoes
Context: Neighborhood Defenders; zoning control vs. state preemption.
Prompt: How might local governments or "neighborhood defenders" attempt to veto or block your policy implementation? What state-level preemption or incentive is required to overcome this?
Week 7: Decisions & Public Finance
Context: Stone Ch. 11. California's budget process and fiscal volatility.
Prompt: Conduct a rough Cost-Benefit Analysis of your policy. How will you fund this reform during a cycle of state budget deficits?
Week 8: Rules & The Courts (CEQA)
Context: Stone Ch. 13. CEQA lawsuits and environmental rules.
Prompt: How could existing rules (like CEQA) be weaponized in the courts to delay or kill your policy? Suggest a statutory "rule" change to insulate your reform from endless litigation.
Week 9: Welfare & Security (Housing)
Context: Stone Ch. 4. Defining need and security in public policy.
Prompt: Draft a formal Public Comment letter (to a committee or agency) that argues for your policy not just as an economic necessity, but as a fundamental issue of human welfare and security.
Week 10: Liberty & Mandates (Climate)
Context: Stone Ch. 5. Negative vs. positive liberty in the energy transition.
Prompt: Does your policy require a government mandate that infringes on "negative liberty" (the right to be left alone)? How will you defend this mandate as a necessary step for "positive liberty" (the capacity to thrive)?
Week 11: Rights & Powers (Water Politics)
Context: Stone Ch. 14. The creation and enforcement of positive and normative rights.
Prompt: Does your policy aim to establish a new legal "right" for Californians? How will this right be enforced, and who holds the power to enforce it?
Week 12: Media Toolkit (Infrastructure)
Context: High-Speed Rail and the challenge of sustaining public support for megaprojects.
Prompt: Draft an outline for an Op-Ed or a Press Release. What is your headline? What are the three core talking points you want the media to amplify?
Week 13: Persuasion & Mobilization (Inequality)
Context: Stone Ch. 10. Labor, inequality, and the "New Margins."
Prompt: Design a grassroots mobilization strategy. Who are your natural allies (unions, tenant groups, business chambers) and how will you persuade them to march, call, or lobby for your bill?
Week 14: Statutory Drafting (Social Welfare)
Context: Translating broad ideas into strict statutory language.
Prompt: Draft the exact statutory language (a mock bill or amendment text) required to enact your policy. Be precise about funding mechanisms, enforcement, and definitions.
Week 15: Oral Testimony (Criminal Justice)
Context: Stone Ch. 8 & 12. Persuasion in committee hearings.
Prompt: Outline your 3-minute oral testimony for a legislative committee hearing. What is your hook? How will you pre-empt the opposition's strongest argument?
Week 16: Final Reflection
Context: Navigating political power and finalizing the advocacy portfolio.
Prompt: Reflect on the simulation. What was the most difficult structural barrier to passing your policy in California? How did your strategy evolve over the past 16 weeks?
